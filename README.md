## The one-paragraph version

We run a 24/7 emergency home repair service that gets to a homeowner's door within
90 minutes of a burst pipe, storm hit, break-in, roof leak, or failed structural
element. Emergency work is real revenue — much of it billed to homeowners insurance —
but its strategic job is **customer acquisition**. Every emergency call puts us inside
a damaged house at the exact moment the owner has to decide who rebuilds it. We
convert roughly one in four of the calls that involve real damage into a full
carpentry or remodeling contract at 4–20x the ticket size. Competitors sell remodeling and hope the phone
rings. We buy the relationship at the moment of crisis and own the rebuild.

**That's the whole business. Everything in these documents serves that funnel.**

## The four numbers that decide whether this works

Everything else is commentary. Track these weekly from day one.

| Metric | Target | Why it decides the business |
|---|---|---|
| **Call answer rate** | ≥ 98% live, < 3 rings | Emergency callers dial the next number in 90 seconds. A missed call is a lost customer *and* a lost rebuild. |
| **Emergency → rebuild conversion** | ≥ 25% of *qualified* calls | This is the entire thesis. Below 15%, we're just an expensive handyman service. |
| **Gross margin** | ≥ 42% blended | Below 35% no amount of revenue saves us. Contractors die at high revenue and low margin. |
| **Crew utilization (billable hrs ÷ paid hrs)** | ≥ 70% | Every point below 70% comes straight out of net profit. This is where most shops bleed out. |

## Assumptions this plan is built on

Called out plainly so you can correct any that are wrong before you take this to the investor:

1. **State-agnostic.** Licensing, contractor registration, and bond thresholds vary by
   state. Section 8 gives a fill-in-the-blank compliance checklist plus exactly where
   to look up your state's rules. **Do this before the first job over $500.**
2. **The investor funds, the operators run.** Capital comes from the investor; day-to-day
   control sits with the two operating members. Section 2 shows how to paper this so it
   stays true.
3. **"He has the workers" is not yet a legal arrangement.** Section 2.5 is the most
   important thing in this plan to resolve before you file anything. Read it first if
   you read nothing else tonight.
4. **Service area:** one metro, ~30-minute drive radius from the shop. Emergency response
   is a geography business — you cannot promise 90 minutes across two counties.
5. **Year 1 base case is ~$780K revenue at ~3% net.** That is a *realistic* first-year
   contractor outcome, not a pitch number. Upside is in Year 2 when overhead stops scaling.

## Fastest path to revenue if you only have this week

1. File the LLC + get the EIN (same day, online). → §2.2
2. Bind General Liability + Commercial Auto + Workers' Comp. **You cannot work without
   these.** → §8.2
3. Set up the 24/7 answering service and one phone number. → §4.1
4. Stock one emergency response vehicle from the load list. → §4.3
5. Print the Work Authorization form. **No signature, no work.** → [forms/](forms/)
6. Claim Google Business Profile and turn on emergency-intent search ads. → §7.2

Everything else can follow. Those six things are the difference between a business and
an idea.
