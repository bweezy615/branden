## Week 0 — Before anything else

**One conversation, and it happens first.**

| # | Task | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | **Ask the investor: are the workers going on our payroll or staying on his?** Get a clear answer. → [§2.5](02-formation-and-ownership.md#25-the-worker-problem--read-this-first) | A + B + INV |
| 0.2 | **Confirm the deal structure: equity with preferred return, or debt?** → [§2.3](02-formation-and-ownership.md#23-ownership-structure) | A + B + INV |
| 0.3 | Confirm capital amount, timing, and how it gets funded | A + B + INV |
| 0.4 | Agree who does what day-to-day, in writing | A + B |
| 0.5 | Define the service area — a 30-minute drive radius, drawn on a map | A + B |

> **Do not file the LLC before 0.1 and 0.2 are answered.** Both change the operating
> agreement, and amending one after the fact costs more than getting it right now.

## The five things that matter most in 90 days

Everything above is execution detail. If you get only five things right:

1. **Answer every single call, 24/7.** It is the cheapest lever in the business and worth
   ~$61,000/year against a $250/month cost. Nothing else comes close.
2. **Get the worker classification right before you start.** It is the only item here that
   can reach through the LLC and take your personal assets.
3. **Job cost every job, every week, from job number one.** You cannot fix estimating you
   aren't measuring, and 90 days of unmeasured jobs is 90 days of repeating the same
   mistake at increasing volume.
4. **Run the conversion bridge on every emergency call.** Document, follow up the next
   morning, meet the adjuster, present the rebuild. This is the entire thesis and it is
   a behavior, not a strategy.
5. **Don't spend the working capital on equipment.** You will be tempted around week 3.
   The model shows a cumulative deficit trough around –$38K in month 5, and cash runs
   worse than that. That runway is what keeps the company alive long enough to work.
