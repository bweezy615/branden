# 7. Marketing & Sales

## 7.1 Positioning

**The line that does all the work:**

> **"When your house can't wait until Monday."**

That single sentence carries the whole differentiator. Every competitor in the market
either answers the phone at 11pm *or* can build you a kitchen. We do both, with our own
crews, under one license and one insurance policy.

**Two audiences, two messages, one company** (this is why we run two DBAs — see
[§2.1](02-formation-and-ownership.md#21-naming)):

| | Emergency audience | Remodeling audience |
|---|---|---|
| **State of mind** | Panicked, deciding in 90 seconds | Researching, deciding over weeks |
| **What they need to hear** | *"We answer 24/7. On site in 90 minutes. Licensed and insured."* | *"Craftsmanship, clear pricing, on schedule, and we're the ones who fix it if something goes wrong."* |
| **Proof they look for** | Reviews, real photos, a person who answers | Portfolio, references, warranty, permits pulled |
| **Where they look** | Google search, maps, on the phone | Google, Instagram, referrals, neighbors |
| **Decision time** | Under 5 minutes | 2–8 weeks |

**Never compete on price.** You are selling response time, licensure, insurance, and the
fact that a real company will still exist in two years when the deck needs a warranty
repair. When a prospect says you're expensive, the answer is *"we are — here's what that
buys you,"* not a discount. Discounting to win work is how contractors end the year busy
and broke.

## 7.2 Channels and budget

Year 1 marketing budget: **$46,800** (6.0% of revenue). Allocation:

| Channel | Year 1 | Share | Serves |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Google Local Services Ads + Search | $18,000 | 38% | **Emergency — highest intent, pay-per-lead** |
| Google Business Profile + local SEO | $4,800 | 10% | Both — compounds, near-zero marginal cost |
| Website + landing pages | $6,000 | 13% | Both |
| Vehicle wraps & yard signs | $5,500 | 12% | Both — one-time spend, years of impressions |
| Direct mail (storm-triggered, targeted) | $5,000 | 11% | Emergency + neighborhood saturation |
| Social (Instagram/Facebook, project content) | $3,500 | 7% | Remodeling |
| Referral & partner program | $2,500 | 5% | Both — highest ROI channel |
| Print, door hangers, community sponsorship | $1,500 | 3% | Local awareness |
| **Total** | **$46,800** | | |

### Where to actually start

**1. Google Business Profile — free, day one, highest priority.** Emergency searches are
overwhelmingly local and map-pack driven. Claim it, complete every field, add real project
photos weekly, list 24/7 hours (this is a genuine ranking and click advantage — most
competitors list 8–5), and answer every question. **This is the single most valuable free
asset in a local home-services business.**

**2. Google Local Services Ads (LSA).** Pay per lead, not per click. The "Google
Guaranteed" badge requires background checks and license/insurance verification, which is
a barrier your uninsured competitors can't clear — that's the point. LSAs appear above
everything else on emergency searches.

**3. Search ads on emergency-intent keywords.** Bid on the language people actually type
at midnight: *emergency plumber near me open now, burst pipe repair 24 hour, board up
service, roof leak emergency, water damage repair tonight, tree fell on house*. These
convert at extraordinary rates because intent is absolute. Bid aggressively — a $60 click
that produces a $2,082 expected-value call is a bargain.

**4. Vehicle wraps.** A wrapped truck in a driveway at 2am is seen by every neighbor the
next morning — in a neighborhood that just experienced the same storm. Cheapest impressions
you will ever buy.

**5. Storm-triggered direct mail.** After a significant weather event, mail the affected
zip codes within 72 hours. Timing is everything; a generic postcard is wasted, a postcard
that arrives three days after the hail is not.

### Track everything

- **Unique tracking phone numbers per channel.** Without this you're guessing, and you
  will keep funding the channel that feels productive instead of the one that is.
- Ask every caller how they found you, and log it in the CRM.
- Review cost-per-lead and cost-per-*booked-job* monthly. Cut what doesn't convert within
  60 days and move the money to what does.

## 7.3 Reviews and reputation

**Reviews are the top of the entire funnel.** They drive local search ranking, which drives
emergency call volume, which drives rebuild conversion, which is the business. Treat review
generation as an operational process, not a marketing afterthought.

**Target: 50+ Google reviews at 4.7★+ by month 12** — roughly 3 per week.

**The system:**
1. Ask at the moment of peak gratitude. For emergencies, that's the **morning after** —
   not on site while they're still stressed. For projects, at the final walkthrough.
2. **Text a direct review link within 2 hours** of that moment. Email gets ignored; text
   gets a ~30% response rate.
3. Pay the **$25 review bonus** when the review names the technician
   ([§5.2](05-staffing-and-crew.md#variable-compensation)). It makes the whole crew care.
4. **Respond to every review**, positive and negative, within 24 hours.
5. **Answer negative reviews well.** Never argue, never blame the customer. Acknowledge,
   state what you're doing about it, take it offline. A well-handled 2-star review sells
   more work than a 5-star review, because it shows prospects what happens when something
   goes wrong.

**Never buy fake reviews.** Google detects and penalizes them, and a suspended profile
would take out your primary lead source.

## 7.4 Referral and partner channels

The highest-ROI channel in the business and the one that costs the least. Build these
deliberately in the first 90 days.

| Partner | What they give you | What you give them |
|---|---|---|
| **Insurance adjusters** ⭐ | Direct referrals on claims — the highest-value referrals available | Clean documentation, fast response, honest scopes, never an inflated estimate |
| **Insurance agents (local)** | Referrals when a client calls them first after a loss | Reliability, a card they can hand out, no callbacks to them |
| **Plumbers, electricians, HVAC** | They find water and structural damage constantly | Reciprocal referrals + you subcontract them |
| **Roofers** | Interior damage after leaks | You refer full roof replacements to them |
| **Realtors** | Pre-sale repair work, inspection punch lists | Fast turnarounds on tight closing timelines |
| **Property managers** | Steady repeat volume | Priority response, predictable pricing |
| **Restoration franchises** | They mitigate, you rebuild — many subcontract reconstruction | Reliable, licensed carpentry capacity |
| **Past customers** | Referrals + repeat work | $200 referral reward on a signed job |

**On adjusters specifically — this deserves real effort.** Adjusters carry heavy caseloads
and refer to contractors who make their files easy: fast response, complete photo
documentation, properly formatted Xactimate estimates, no scope inflation, no arguing.
Introduce yourself in person. Show up to the inspection. Submit clean paperwork every
single time.

**Three good adjuster relationships are worth more than $30,000 of advertising**, and
they compound — an adjuster who trusts you refers you for years at zero acquisition cost.

## 7.5 The sales process

### Emergency (decided in under 5 minutes)

The sale is won at intake and on site. There is no pitch.
- Answer live in under 3 rings
- Screen for safety, capture details, **state the price out loud**
- Technician calls within 15 minutes with an ETA
- Arrive when you said, in a clean truck and a clean shirt
- Explain what you're doing in plain language before you do it

**That's it.** Competence at 2am is the entire sales presentation. The rebuild sale
happens later, per [§3.3](03-services-and-pricing.md#33-the-bridge-converting-emergency-calls-into-rebuild-contracts).

### Projects (decided over weeks)

1. **Respond to the lead within one business hour.** Speed-to-lead is the strongest
   predictor of close rate in home services. Most competitors take a day.
2. **Site visit — listen more than you talk.** Understand what they actually want, what
   they're worried about, and what they've been told by others.
3. **Discuss budget on the first visit.** Give a range and watch the reaction. An
   estimator who won't talk money until the proposal wastes everyone's time and loses to
   whoever was straight with them.
4. **Deliver the estimate within 3 business days**, always. Contractors lose more work to
   slowness than to price. This is a controllable variable and most of your competitors
   are bad at it.
5. **Present in person or on a call — never by bare email.** Walk them through scope,
   exclusions, schedule, and payment terms. An emailed PDF gets compared on price alone,
   which is the only comparison you lose.
6. **Ask for the signature.** Directly. *"If this looks right, I can get you on the
   schedule for the week of the 14th — should I write it up?"*
7. **Follow up at 3 days, 10 days, and 30 days.** Then quarterly. Remodeling decisions
   stall for months, and the contractor still politely present when the decision unsticks
   wins the job.

### Estimate presentation — what to include

- **Detailed scope** — what's included, stated so a non-builder understands it
- **Explicit exclusions** — what is *not* included. This prevents the most common source
  of disputes and it makes you look more competent, not less.
- **Allowances** clearly stated for anything not yet selected (fixtures, tile, hardware)
- **Schedule** — realistic start and duration, with weather caveats
- **Payment terms** and the change-order policy, stated before they sign, not after
- **Warranty** terms
- **Proof** — license number, insurance summary, references, photos of similar work

**Give one clear price, not a menu of three tiers.** Tiered pricing works for products,
not for construction — it invites shopping the scope and signals that your first number
was negotiable.

## 7.6 Content that actually generates work

Skip the generic blog. Two things reliably produce leads in this business:

**1. Real project photos, posted consistently.** Before/during/after on every project.
Post to Google Business Profile and Instagram weekly. This is the remodeling portfolio and
it costs nothing but the discipline to take the photos you're already required to take for
[§4.4](04-operations-manual.md#44-documentation-standard).

**2. Emergency preparedness content, timed to the season.** *"Where is your water shut-off
and can you reach it in the dark?"* · *"How to protect your pipes before a freeze"* ·
*"What to do in the first 10 minutes of a roof leak"* · *"What your homeowners policy
actually covers after a storm."*

This content is genuinely useful, it earns local links and shares, and — most importantly —
it puts your name in front of homeowners *before* the emergency. People call the company
whose name they already know when the ceiling starts dripping.
