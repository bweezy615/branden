## 4.1 Intake — the most important 90 seconds

**The single rule: the phone gets answered by a human, 24 hours a day, every day.**

An emergency caller who reaches voicemail is gone in under 90 seconds. They are not
leaving a message; they are dialing the next result. Every missed call costs an $850
mitigation ticket *and* a 25% shot at a $19,000 rebuild — call it **$5,600 of expected
value per missed call.** A 24/7 answering service costs $200–$450/month. The math is
not close.

### Intake stack

| Layer | Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Business hours (7a–6p)** | Office coordinator | Live answer, full intake, dispatch |
| **2. After hours** | 24/7 live answering service | Live answer, script-driven intake, escalate to on-call |
| **3. Overflow / simultaneous calls** | Answering service | Never let a second caller hit voicemail |
| **4. Web & text** | CRM with auto-response | Instant acknowledgment, then human follow-up in <15 min |

**Choose an answering service that specializes in home services / restoration.** They
already know the triage vocabulary. Give them the script below, a call-outcome report
requirement, and a monthly review of recordings. Audit them — a bad answering service
quietly destroys the business and you'll never see it in your own call log.

### Emergency intake script

Give this to the answering service verbatim and post it at the office desk.

> **1. Answer within 3 rings:**
> *"[COMPANY] emergency repair, this is [Name] — are you and everyone in the home safe
> right now?"*
>
> **2. Safety screen. If ANY of these — stop and redirect immediately:**
> - Smell of gas → *"Get everyone out of the house right now and call 911 and your gas
>   utility from outside. Call us back once they've cleared it and we'll handle the repair."*
> - Active fire or smoke → **911.**
> - Sparking, arcing, or water contacting electrical → *"Shut the main breaker off if you
>   can do it safely. If you can't reach it safely, get out and call the power company."*
> - Structure actively collapsing / audible cracking → *"Get everyone out now and call 911."*
>
> **3. Capture — every field, no skipping:**
> - Name, callback number, **service address**
> - What happened, and when did it start
> - Is water still running? *(If yes: "Do you know where your main shut-off is? Can you
>   get to it? I'll stay on the line.")* — **stopping the water is worth more to them
>   than anything else you say tonight**
> - Is the home secure? Any opening to the outside?
> - Is anyone in the home elderly, disabled, an infant, or medically vulnerable? *(Escalates priority)*
> - Homeowner or renter? *(Renter → we need the owner's authorization)*
> - Have you contacted your insurance company yet?
>
> **4. Set expectations — say the numbers out loud:**
> *"Here's what happens next. Our technician will call you within 15 minutes and be on
> site within 90 minutes. Our after-hours dispatch fee is $275, which covers the first
> hour on site, and anything beyond that is billed hourly — the tech will walk you
> through the full cost before any work starts. Does that work?"*
>
> **5. Confirm and dispatch:** *"You're confirmed. [Tech name] is on the way. If anything
> changes, call this same number — someone's here 24/7."*

**Why the price is disclosed on the phone, always:** it eliminates the doorstep argument,
it filters people who were never going to pay, and it makes you the honest company. The
ones who balk at $275 at 2am were going to be a collections problem at 2pm.

### Triage priority

| Priority | Response | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Immediate** | Dispatch now, on site < 90 min | Active water, open structure, unsecured after break-in, tree through roof, vulnerable occupant |
| **P2 — Same day** | Dispatch within 4 hours | Contained leak stopped, damaged but secure, failed door that still locks |
| **P3 — Next business day** | Schedule for morning | Cosmetic damage, non-urgent repair, secure and dry |
| **Refer out** | Referral within 5 minutes | Gas, main electrical, sewage, abatement, in-progress collapse |

**A P3 dispatched at 1am is a margin loss and a tired crew.** Train the answering service
to distinguish. "Urgent to the homeowner" and "emergency" are different things, and the
tech's phone call within 15 minutes is where that gets sorted — kindly.

## 4.2 Dispatch & the on-call rotation

**On-call structure:**
- One lead tech + one helper on call per week, rotating.
- On-call runs Friday 5pm → Friday 5pm.
- **On-call stipend: $200/week** whether or not they get called out, plus full hourly
  (at premium rate, 2-hour minimum) for every call taken.
- On-call tech keeps the stocked response vehicle at home. This is what makes 90 minutes
  achievable — nobody drives to a shop first.
- A designated backup is named every week. If primary doesn't acknowledge dispatch in
  10 minutes, the answering service escalates to backup, then to an owner.

**The stipend is not optional and it is not generous.** Being tethered to a phone for
168 hours has a real cost. Underpay it and your best carpenter quits or stops answering,
and either way the 90-minute promise dies.

**Dispatch sequence, target 15 minutes call-to-rolling:**

```
0:00  Call received, intake completed
0:03  Answering service pages on-call tech (call + text with address & summary)
0:08  Tech acknowledges (10-min escalation timer to backup)
0:12  Tech calls homeowner directly — confirms details, re-confirms pricing,
      gives an ETA, and coaches them on immediate steps (shut-off, move
      valuables, don't walk on a sagging ceiling)
0:15  Rolling
≤1:30 On site
```

**That 12-minute homeowner call is a competitive weapon.** No competitor does it. It
converts panic into confidence, it filters out the jobs that aren't real, and it means
the tech arrives to a customer who is already glad they chose you.

## 4.3 The response vehicle

The vehicle *is* the emergency division. If the tech has to stop at Home Depot at 1am,
the response promise is broken.

### Standard load list

**Securing & dry-in**
- 6–8 sheets ½" OSB/plywood (pre-cut: 4×4, 4×8, 2×4 sections)
- 4 tarps (20×30) + 4 tarps (12×16), heavy mil
- Furring strips / 1×3 for tarp battens, sandbags
- Roofing nails, cap nails, screws, ring-shank nails
- Plastic sheeting (6 mil, 20×100), duct tape, painter's tape
- Zip poles and zipper doors for containment

**Water**
- Portable water extractor
- 2 dehumidifiers (LGR preferred)
- 4 air movers
- Moisture meter (pin and pinless) + thermo-hygrometer
- Wet/dry vac, squeegees, mops, buckets
- Nitrile gloves, N95s, Tyvek suits, boot covers

**Structural & carpentry**
- Assorted 2×4, 2×6, 2×8 (8' and 10')
- Adjustable steel shoring posts (4)
- LVL scrap / blocking stock
- Simpson connectors, structural screws, lag bolts, joist hangers
- Construction adhesive, spray foam

**Plumbing / electrical stop-gap**
- SharkBite fittings & caps (½", ¾"), PEX, pipe cutter
- Push-to-connect valves, hose clamps, pipe repair tape
- Wire nuts, electrical tape, temporary junction boxes
- **For stopping damage only** — licensed trades do the permanent repair

**Power & light**
- Generator (3500W+), fuel can
- 2 LED work lights on stands, headlamps, 100' extension cords
- Battery-powered everything (impact, circ saw, recip saw, drill) + spare batteries

**Site & safety**
- First aid kit, fire extinguisher, eye wash
- Fall protection harness + rope grab, ladder (24' extension + 6' step)
- Cones, caution tape, floor protection runners, shoe covers
- Camera/tablet, printed forms, tape measure, chalk, marker

**Customer-facing**
- Clean company shirts (spare set)
- Business cards, door hangers, branded folder for leaving documentation

### Vehicle rules

- **The on-call tech restocks before going off rotation.** Signed restock checklist
  handed to the coordinator. No signature, no on-call stipend paid.
- Truck inventory is audited monthly by an owner.
- Fuel above ½ tank at all times when on call.
- Vehicle is wrapped and clean. It is a rolling billboard parked in the driveway of a
  neighborhood full of homeowners who just watched something bad happen to a neighbor.

## 4.4 Documentation standard

**Every emergency job, no exceptions, no discretion.** This is what gets insurance claims
paid and what defends you when someone claims you caused the damage.

**On arrival, before touching anything:**
1. Photo of the house front with the address number visible
2. Wide shots of every affected room from all four corners
3. Close-ups of all damage
4. Photo of the source (the failed pipe, the hole, the broken window)
5. Moisture readings — photograph the meter *against the surface being read*
6. Photo of anything already damaged that we did not cause (**this protects you**)

**During:**
7. Progress photos of each mitigation step
8. Photo of every piece of equipment placed, with its location
9. Photo of any hidden condition discovered (rot, mold, prior bad repair, no header)

**On completion:**
10. Wide shots of every affected area, same angles as the "before"
11. Photo of the completed securing / tarp / shoring
12. Photo of the signed Work Authorization
13. Photo of the equipment left on site and the written pickup date

**Also captured:** arrival and departure timestamps, all materials used with quantities,
all labor hours by person, a written narrative of what was found and what was done, and
weather conditions if the loss is weather-related.

> **Rule: if it isn't photographed, it didn't happen and it won't be paid.** An adjuster
> denying a line item is not asking whether you did the work — they're asking whether you
> can prove it. Techs who won't document don't stay on the emergency rotation.

The whole package goes to the homeowner the next morning ([§3.3](03-services-and-pricing.md#33-the-bridge-converting-emergency-calls-into-rebuild-contracts))
and into the job file.

## 4.5 Project job flow (Division B)

Every project moves through nine gates. **A job cannot advance until its gate is closed.**

| Gate | What happens | Gate closes when |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Lead** | Logged in CRM within 15 min, contacted within 1 business hour | Appointment set or lead disqualified |
| **2. Site visit & scope** | Measure, photograph, identify hidden risk, discuss budget range on site | Scope documented, budget range verbally agreed |
| **3. Estimate** | Bottom-up build per [§3.2](03-services-and-pricing.md#pricing-method-fixed-price-built-bottom-up), written material & sub quotes | Estimate delivered within **3 business days** |
| **4. Contract** | Written contract: scope, exclusions, price, schedule, payment terms, change order policy, warranty, right to cancel | Signed + deposit **cleared** |
| **5. Pre-production** | Permits pulled, materials ordered, subs scheduled, crew assigned, job number issued | Materials confirmed & permit in hand |
| **6. Mobilization** | Protection down, dumpster set, site rules reviewed with homeowner | Mobilization draw collected |
| **7. Production** | Daily log, daily photos, weekly job cost review, inspections scheduled | Rough-in inspection passed |
| **8. Finish & punch** | Walkthrough with homeowner, punch list written and dated | Every punch item signed off |
| **9. Close** | Final invoice, lien release exchanged, warranty issued, **review requested**, HomeGuard offered | Final payment cleared |

### Gate rules that are not negotiable

- **No work starts before a signed contract and a cleared deposit.** Not "the check is
  in the mail." *Cleared.*
- **No change without a signed change order at a stated price.** The verbal "while you're
  here, can you also…" is the single largest source of margin loss in remodeling. The
  answer is always: *"Absolutely — let me write that up, it'll be about $X, and I'll get
  it to you this afternoon."* → [forms/change-order.md](../forms/change-order.md)
- **The daily log is filled out every day by the crew lead.** Who was there, hours,
  what got done, what's blocked, weather, deliveries, visitors. Ten minutes. It wins
  disputes and it makes job costing possible. → [forms/daily-job-log.md](../forms/daily-job-log.md)
- **Permits get pulled.** Working without a permit voids the homeowner's insurance on
  that work, exposes you to license discipline, and creates a title problem that surfaces
  when they sell. Structural, electrical, plumbing, decks over a height threshold, and
  egress changes essentially always require one. When in doubt, call the building
  department — they will tell you for free.
- **Punch list gets a date.** Open punch lists are how final payments die. Walk it,
  write it, date it, finish it inside 5 business days.

## 4.6 Money controls

Profitable contractors go broke. These are the controls that prevent it.

### Job costing — every job, every week

Every job gets a **job number** at Gate 5, and every dollar of labor, material, sub, and
equipment gets coded to it. Weekly, compare estimated vs. actual by category.

```
Job #2026-0148 — Henderson Deck
                      Estimated    Actual    Variance
  Labor (hrs)              96         112      -16 hrs  ⚠
  Labor ($)           $ 4,240     $ 4,947     -$  707
  Materials           $ 6,800     $ 7,150     -$  350
  Subs                $ 1,200     $ 1,200     $     0
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Direct cost         $12,240     $13,297     -$1,057
  Contract price      $19,500     $19,500
  Gross margin           37.2%       31.8%     -5.4pts  ⚠
```

**Then ask why, every time.** Was the estimate wrong, or was the execution wrong? Those
have completely different fixes, and a company that doesn't know which one it has will
keep making the same mistake at increasing volume. Job costing is not accounting — it is
how estimating learns.

### Cash rules

1. **Deposits before mobilization.** No exceptions.
2. **Invoice the day the milestone is hit.** Not Friday. Not month-end. That day. Every
   day of delay is a day of your money funding someone else's house.
3. **Draws stop the job when late.** Written notice, work stops, resume on payment. Put
   it in the contract so it's a term, not a fight.
4. **Materials on the business card, coded to the job**, paid in full monthly. This gives
   30–45 days of free float and a clean audit trail — but only if you never carry a
   balance at 24% interest.
5. **Preserve lien rights on every job.** Many states require a **preliminary notice**
   within a set window (often 20 days from first furnishing) or you lose lien rights
   entirely. Find your state's rule and build it into Gate 5. → [§8.1](08-risk-insurance-compliance.md#81-licensing-the-fill-in-the-blank-checklist)
6. **Collect a lien waiver from every subcontractor** with their payment. Otherwise a sub
   you already paid can lien your customer's house, and you will pay twice.
7. **Set aside taxes weekly**, in a separate account. Pass-through income tax is not
   withheld for you. Roughly 25–30% of distributions.
8. **Nothing over 60 days.** At 30 days: call. At 45: written demand. At 60: lien notice
   or collections. Receivables age into losses fast, and the contractor who is
   uncomfortable asking for money is the one who goes under.

### Weekly financial cadence — 45 minutes, every Monday

- Cash position and next 3 weeks of committed outflows
- Receivables aging
- Job cost variance on every active job
- Backlog: contracted work not yet started (this is your forward revenue)
- Last week's crew utilization

## 4.7 The weekly scorecard

One page. Reviewed by both operators every Monday. If a number is red two weeks running,
it becomes the week's priority — not a topic for later.

| Metric | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Calls received | — | Answering service report |
| **Calls answered live** | **≥ 98%** | Answering service report |
| Avg. call-to-on-site time | ≤ 90 min | Dispatch log |
| Emergency jobs completed | — | Job log |
| Avg. emergency ticket | ≥ $850 | Invoicing |
| Qualified rebuild opportunities *(≈35% of calls)* | — | CRM |
| **Qualified call → estimate delivered** | **≥ 60%** | CRM |
| **Estimate → signed contract** | **≥ 40%** | CRM |
| **Net conversion (qualified → rebuild)** | **≥ 25%** | CRM |
| Estimates delivered within 3 days | 100% | CRM |
| **Crew utilization** | **≥ 70%** | Payroll vs. job hours |
| **Gross margin, jobs closed this week** | **≥ 42%** | Job costing |
| Receivables > 45 days | $0 | Accounting |
| Callbacks / warranty visits | ≤ 5% of jobs | Job log |
| New reviews | ≥ 3/week | Google |
| Safety incidents | 0 | Incident log |

**The two-stage conversion split matters.** "We're not converting" has two very different
causes: not *asking* (emergency → estimate) or not *closing* (estimate → contract). The
first is a technician training problem, the second is a pricing or presentation problem.
Measuring them together tells you nothing actionable.

## 4.8 Technology stack

Keep it small. Most contractors buy six tools, use two, and pay for all of them.

| Function | Tool | Cost/mo | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRM + estimating + scheduling + invoicing | Jobber, Housecall Pro, or ServiceTitan | $150 – $400 | **Day 1** |
| Accounting + job costing | QuickBooks Online Plus | $100 | **Day 1** |
| 24/7 answering service | Home-services specialist | $200 – $450 | **Day 1** |
| Phone system | VoIP with call recording & tracking numbers | $50 | **Day 1** |
| Insurance estimating | **Xactimate** | $150 – $300 | Month 1–2 |
| Photo & job documentation | CompanyCam | $60 | Month 1 |
| Payroll | Gusto or ADP | $80 | **Day 1** |
| E-signature | Built into CRM, or Dropbox Sign | $0 – $25 | **Day 1** |
| **Total** | | **$790 – $1,465/mo** | |

**Set up job costing in QuickBooks before the first invoice.** Retrofitting it later is
brutal and you will not do it, and then you will run a construction company without
knowing which jobs make money.

**Pick one CRM and commit.** Switching CRMs mid-year costs more in lost data and
retraining than any feature difference is worth.

## 4.9 Quality control & warranty

**Three-point quality check on every project:**
1. **Rough-in check** by an owner or lead before anything gets covered
2. **Pre-finish check** before paint and trim
3. **Final walkthrough** with the homeowner, punch list written on the spot

**Warranty:** 1 year on workmanship, manufacturer warranty passed through on materials.
Issue it in writing at close. Some states mandate a longer implied warranty on structural
work — check yours.

**Callback policy:** a warranty callback is scheduled within 3 business days and it is
never argued about in front of the customer. Fix it, then figure out internally whether
it was a crew error, a material failure, or a scope misunderstanding. **Track callbacks
by crew lead** — a callback rate above 5% is a training problem, and it is far cheaper
to train than to lose reviews.

**Reviews:** ask at close, every time, while the goodwill is at its peak. Text a direct
link within 2 hours of final walkthrough. For emergency jobs, ask the morning after —
that is peak gratitude. Reviews are the primary ranking factor in local search, which
means they are the primary driver of emergency call volume, which means they are the
top of the entire funnel. → [§7.3](07-marketing-and-sales.md#73-reviews-and-reputation)
