# 5. Business Plan — The Meta Ads Service

← [30-Day Operating Plan](04-30-day-operating-plan.md) · [Index](README.md)

---

Budget Barn is one client. This document is about whether **selling this to thirty
Budget Barns** is a business.

Short answer: yes, but not because of the first month. The first month is close to
break-even on time. **The business is the renewal.**

---

## 5.1 The thesis

> Local businesses will pay a fixed, modest price to find out whether paid social works
> for them — a question they can't answer alone and can't stop wondering about. The
> test month barely pays. It exists to produce a small number of clients who keep
> paying, at a fraction of the labor, for a year or more.

Every decision in this plan follows from that: price the test to be easy to say yes
to, deliver it well enough that half of them renew, and defend the renewal margin.

## 5.2 Unit economics

### Per client, per month

| | **Month 1 (new)** | **Month 2+ (retained)** |
|---|---|---|
| Client pays | $1,450 | $1,450 |
| Meta media (COGS) | $750 | $850 |
| **Gross profit** | **$700** | **$600** |
| Gross margin | 48% | 41% |
| **Marvo's hours** | **~11** | **~4** |
| **Effective hourly** | **$64** | **$150** |

### Where the 11 hours go in month 1

| Activity | Hours |
|---|---|
| Setup, access, pixel, scoreboard | 2.0 |
| Creative gathering and build | 2.5 |
| Campaign build and launch | 1.5 |
| Daily management across 30 days | 2.5 |
| Reporting (4 weekly + day-14 + final) | 2.0 |
| Renewal conversation | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **11.0** |

Month 2+ drops to ~4 hrs: no setup, no account build, no audience research, and
creative refresh instead of creative from scratch.

**This single row is the whole business model.** $64/hr is a job. $150/hr is a
business. The gap between them is why you tolerate a thin month one.

### Client lifetime value

Assuming **50% renew into month 2**, then **70% month-over-month** after that:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Average client lifespan | ~2.7 months |
| **Gross profit per client (lifetime)** | **~$1,700** |
| Hours per client (lifetime) | ~18 |
| Effective lifetime rate | **~$94/hr** |

**Cost to acquire one client:** ~4 qualified meetings per close, ~1.5 hrs each
including prep and travel, plus ~$25 hard costs (printing, gas, coffee). Call it
**6 hours and $25** — roughly **$325** at a $50/hr shadow rate.

**LTV:CAC ≈ 5:1.** Healthy. Anything above 3:1 is a business worth running.

### The one lever that matters most

| Renewal rate (month 2) | Avg lifespan | LTV (gross) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35% | 2.2 mo | $1,400 | −18% |
| **50%** | **2.7 mo** | **$1,700** | baseline |
| 65% | 3.2 mo | $2,000 | **+18%** |

Fifteen points of renewal rate is worth more than an 18% price increase — and unlike a
price increase, it costs nothing to sell. **Every trust mechanism in
[Section 3](03-trust-and-risk-reversal.md) is really a renewal-rate investment.**

## 5.3 Overhead and break-even

### Monthly fixed costs

| Item | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Design tool (Canva Pro or similar) | $15 |
| Phone / business line share | $30 |
| Invoicing + bookkeeping software | $25 |
| LLC, registered agent, liability insurance (amortized) | $40 |
| Prospecting costs (gas, printing, coffee) | $100 |
| **Total** | **~$210** |

Plus a **3% of revenue reserve** for day-14 refunds, disputed charges, and dead ad
accounts. Treat this as a real cost, not a contingency — you will use it.

### Break-even

**One client per month.** A single $700 gross covers $210 of overhead more than three
times over.

That's a comfortable floor, and it's the honest reason this is a good side business to
start: the downside is you make a few hundred dollars and learn the platform. The
downside is not that you lose money — **provided you never violate the
100%-upfront rule.** That rule is the only thing standing between this business and a
lending business.

## 5.5 The constraints that will actually bite

### Cash and the card

Peak media on the card is **$7,250/month by month 12.** You need:

- A card with a **$15,000–20,000 limit** (headroom, because Meta bills lumpily)
- **Full payoff every month** — you already collected this money, there is nothing to
  finance
- **2% cash back** on that volume ≈ **$1,250/year of free margin.** Worth choosing the
  card deliberately.

**The 100%-upfront rule is a solvency rule, not a preference.** One client who "pays
after" at nine active clients turns you into an unsecured lender with a $750 exposure
and no recourse. Two of them and you're funding strangers' advertising out of savings.

### Time ceiling

At ~11 hrs for new and ~4 hrs for retained, a solo operator hits real quality strain
around **12–15 active clients** — roughly 70–80 delivery hours/month plus sales. Beyond
that, three options and no fourth:

1. **Raise the price.** The obvious first move, and the one most operators avoid too
   long. Once you have named local results, $1,450 → $1,950 is defensible and costs
   you a small number of the most price-sensitive clients — who are also the ones who
   churn anyway.
2. **Specialize.** Ten clients in the *same vertical* is dramatically less work than
   ten in ten verticals — creative, audiences, and benchmarks all transfer. This is the
   highest-leverage move available and it's free.
3. **Hire.** A part-time operator for build-and-report work at $25–35/hr. Only after
   the pipeline is genuinely reliable — hiring into an uncertain pipeline is how small
   service businesses die.

### Platform risk

- **Ad account restrictions happen, sometimes for no reason.** Keep a backup Business
  Manager. Never promise a launch date you can't control.
- **Meta changes pricing, tooling, and policy constantly.** Never quote thresholds or
  minimums from memory — check Ads Manager before every proposal.
- **A single client's policy violation can taint associated assets.** This is a strong
  additional reason clients own their own Business Manager and you connect as a
  partner: their problems stay theirs.

### Reputation risk

In a local market, reputation is the entire distribution channel. One owner who feels
misled tells everyone at the chamber breakfast. This is why the honest-failure protocol
in [Section 3.4](03-trust-and-risk-reversal.md#34-when-it-fails) is a business
requirement rather than a nicety — **you are selling a product that fails a meaningful
share of the time, and the only sustainable version of that is one where failed clients
still speak well of you.**

## 5.6 Where the next 32 clients come from

Ranked by cost per client, cheapest first.

1. **Referrals from happy clients.** Ask directly at the day-26 renewal, when the
   result is fresh: *"Who else around here should be doing this?"* Free, highest
   conversion, and it compounds. **Should be 40%+ of new business by month 6.**
2. **Referrals from honest failures.** Genuinely a channel. Ask anyway.
3. **The Meta Ad Library as a prospecting tool.** Look up who's already advertising in
   the area — those businesses have proven they'll pay for ads. Then look up who *isn't*
   in a category where competitors are: that's a business losing ground and able to
   see it. This is the single best free prospecting list available and almost nobody
   uses it.
4. **Walk in.** Local retail and service businesses, physical door, in person. Low tech,
   works, and Budget Barn is the proof of concept.
5. **Run your own ads.** Only after you have three named local results to show.
   Advertising your ad service with no results is the weakest possible ad.
6. **Chamber / BNI / local business groups.** Slow to start, then steady. Worth it in a
   small market.

### The pitch that gets you in the door

Not "do you want Facebook ads." Instead:

> "I pulled up the public ad library for [town] — three of your competitors are running
> ads right now and you're not in there at all. Wanted to show you what they're
> running, it takes five minutes."

That's a free, specific, genuinely useful thing to say. It gets meetings.

## 5.7 The four numbers to track weekly

Put these on one sheet from day one. Everything else is commentary.

| # | Number | Target | If it slips |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gross margin per client-month** | ≥ 45% | Media is eating you — raise price or cut spend, don't absorb it |
| 2 | **Hours per retained client** | ≤ 4 | Scope creep. Reset expectations or reprice. |
| 3 | **Month-2 renewal rate** | ≥ 50% | The business isn't compounding — fix delivery or fix the scoreboard |
| 4 | **Cash collected before launch** | 100% | Stop everything. This is the one that ends the business. |

## 5.8 First 90 days

| Weeks | Do this |
|---|---|
| **1–2** | Close Budget Barn. Set up the card, LLC, invoicing, the three forms in [`forms/`](forms/). Run the account properly. |
| **3–6** | Deliver Budget Barn flawlessly. Over-communicate. Treat it as the case study it is — this one client determines how fast everything else moves. |
| **7–8** | Renew Budget Barn on day 26. Ask for two referrals. Write up the result as a one-page anonymized case study. |
| **9–12** | Ad Library prospecting: build a list of 30 local businesses, 10 walk-ins/week. Target 2 new clients closed. |

**The single most important thing in the first 90 days is that Budget Barn is
delighted with how they were treated — separately from whether the ads worked.** That's
what produces the referrals, and referrals are the difference between this model
working and it being a grind of cold walk-ins forever.
