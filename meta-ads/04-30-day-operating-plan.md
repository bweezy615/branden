# 4. The 30-Day Operating Plan

---

What Marvo actually does after the check clears. Built for the **$1,450 / $750 media**
tier at roughly **$25/day**.

**Total time: ~11 hours across 30 days.** Front-loaded — about 6 of those hours are in
the first five days.

---

## Days −5 to 0 — Setup (≈4 hrs)

Nothing runs until every box is checked. A campaign launched on a half-built account
wastes the client's media and your credibility simultaneously.

| # | Task | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Money cleared** | — | Full $1,450 in the account. Non-negotiable gate. |
| 2 | **Scoreboard signed** | 30m | [Form](forms/scoreboard-agreement.md). Do this on a call, not email — the conversation is the value. |
| 3 | **Client creates Business Manager** | 20m | Walk them through it live. `business.facebook.com`. Their name, their ownership. |
| 4 | **Ad account created inside it** | 10m | Correct time zone and currency — **these cannot be changed later.** |
| 5 | **Marvo added as Partner (admin)** | 5m | Partner access only. **Never take their password.** |
| 6 | **Marvo's payment method added** | 5m | Plus a backup card. Call the card issuer about incoming small recurring charges. |
| 7 | **Page connected, Instagram linked** | 15m | Confirm the page info is actually correct — hours, address, phone. Often it isn't. |
| 8 | **Pixel installed** *(if they have a site)* | 30m | Verify it fires. If no site, skip — optimize for messages or calls instead. |
| 9 | **Creative gathered** | 90m | See below. This is the real work. |
| 10 | **Inbound plan agreed** | 15m | Who answers messages, how fast, and how a walk-in gets attributed. Put it in the scoreboard. |

### Creative — where the month is won or lost

The targeting is not the hard part anymore; Meta's system is good at finding people.
**The creative and the offer are the variables that actually move results.**

Get from Budget Barn:
- **Real photos of the actual place, product, and people.** Phone photos of the real
  thing beat stock images and beat polished graphics, consistently, for local business.
- **One genuinely good offer.** Not "come visit us." Something with a reason to act:
  a specific deal, a specific new arrival, a specific event, a deadline.
- **A short video if they'll do one.** Even 15 seconds of the owner talking to the
  camera. It routinely outperforms everything else for local businesses and it costs
  nothing to make.

Build **4 ad variations** that differ on real dimensions, not cosmetics:

| # | Variation | Testing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Owner/staff on camera, plain talk | Does a human face carry it? |
| 2 | Product/inventory hero shot | Does the merchandise sell itself? |
| 3 | Offer-forward, deal in the first line | Is price the lever? |
| 4 | Social proof — a real customer or review | Does credibility carry it? |

**3 audiences:**

1. **Local broad** — radius around the store, minimal other targeting. Let Meta's
   system do its job. Often wins, and most people skip it because it feels lazy.
2. **Local + interest** — same radius, layered with 2–3 interests that genuinely match
   their customer.
3. **Warm** — page/Instagram engagers, plus a website-visitor audience if the pixel
   has traffic. Small, but usually the cheapest results in the account.

**Objective:** whatever the scoreboard's primary metric is. Messages, calls, website
traffic, or store visits. **Never optimize for engagement or reach** — those are the
metrics that made "boosting posts" useless.

## Week 1 (Days 1–7) — Launch and leave it alone (≈1.5 hrs)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Launch. Confirm delivery is actually happening, spend is pacing, nothing's rejected. |
| 2 | 10-minute check: delivery, obvious breakage, nothing else. |
| 3–6 | **Do not touch it.** Daily 5-minute glance for breakage only. |
| 7 | First weekly report. Framed as "here's what we're seeing," not "here's the result." |

**The discipline:** every ad set restarts its learning phase when you make a
significant edit, and edits made on thin early data are guesses that cost real money.
Meta's system needs roughly a week of clean signal.

**This is the single biggest reason DIY attempts fail** — the owner panics on day three,
changes the audience, and permanently resets the clock. Say so in the week-1 report; it
turns your inactivity into a visible professional decision rather than an absence.

> "Week one is deliberately hands-off. Every change resets the system's learning, so
> anything I 'fixed' this week would cost us more than it fixed. Here's the raw data —
> it's early and it's noisy, and that's expected."

## Week 2 (Days 8–14) — Read the signal and hold the checkpoint (≈2 hrs)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 8–10 | First real read. Which creative and audience are separating from the pack? |
| 10–12 | First permitted intervention: pause anything clearly dead. Nothing else. |
| **14** | **The checkpoint sit-down.** In person if possible. |

### The day-14 checkpoint

The most important client interaction of the month. Fifteen minutes.

1. **Show the actual dashboard.** Not a designed report. The real screen. It reads as
   confidence, and it's faster.
2. **Compare against the scoreboard.** Are we tracking toward the number we agreed on?
3. **Say your honest read.** On pace / behind but fixable / not working.
4. **Offer the exit, out loud, unprompted.** Even when it's going well:
   > "We said day fourteen was your out. I don't think you should take it — here's why
   > — but it's yours if you want it."

   Offering an exit you don't want them to take is the strongest trust move in the
   entire engagement. It costs nothing and it's remembered.
5. **Attach the Meta billing receipts.**

---

## Week 3 (Days 15–21) — Consolidate (≈2 hrs)

The winners are visible now. Act on them.

- **Kill the losers.** Pause the bottom half of creative and any audience that isn't
  producing.
- **Consolidate budget into what's working.** This is where most of the month's actual
  results get made — the surviving ads now get 2–3× the daily spend.
- **Iterate the winner.** If variation 2 is winning, build one more like it. Don't
  introduce something unrelated; you're refining now, not exploring.
- **Weekly report**, with a specific note on what changed and why.

---

## Week 4 (Days 22–30) — Scale, report, renew (≈1.5 hrs)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 22–25 | Full budget behind the winner. Watch for fatigue (rising cost per result, falling CTR). |
| **26** | **Renewal conversation — while ads are still live.** |
| 27–29 | Run it out. Build the final report. |
| 30 | Final report delivered, plus the honest recommendation. |

### Why day 26, not day 30

On day 26 the ads are running, the numbers are on the screen, and there's momentum in
the room. On day 30 everything has gone quiet and you're asking a business owner to
restart something that already stopped. **The gap is where renewals die.**

Day-26 script:

> "We're four days out. Where it landed is [X] against the [N] we agreed on. Now that
> we know [the specific thing that worked], month two is a completely different
> starting point — I'm not testing anymore, I'm just running the thing that works.
>
> Same price. And I'll put an extra hundred of it into the ads since I don't have to
> spend the month figuring out what wins. Want me to keep it going without a gap, or
> do you want to sit with the final report first?"

### The final report — one page, plain English

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| What we set out to do | The scoreboard number, restated |
| What happened | Actual results against it |
| What it cost | Total media spent, cost per result, **receipts attached** |
| What won | The specific creative and audience, with the numbers |
| What didn't | Said plainly. Don't hide the losers — it's evidence the test was real. |
| **My honest recommendation** | Continue / adjust and continue / stop. **Say "stop" when it's "stop."** |

No jargon. No 20-page deck. The owner should be able to read it in three minutes and
explain it to their spouse.
